Everything above still applies. Cover the same requirements. Change only the surface - but change it in
EVERY company, not just the consulting one. A recruiter reads these side by side; the older, more
distinctive roles are exactly where identical phrasing is noticed.

- Do not reuse any figure below, and do not put the same KIND of figure on the same kind of work.
  If another candidate measured a percentage on a deployment bullet, measure yours with a duration, a
  throughput, a cost or a count. Different digits in the same unit still reads as one template: three
  near-identical percentages sitting in the same slot is the failure this rule exists to stop. Your
  four figures keep their four different kinds.
- Do not reuse any phrasing listed below, whatever verb you put in front of it. Swapping the
  opening verb while keeping the rest of the sentence is not variation.
- Give each company a different NUMBER of bullets than the other candidates did, ordered differently.
- Where a company has several projects or engagements, tell them in a different ORDER, and open the
  section with a different one than the other candidates led on. When two candidates share a client
  industry there is no way to change WHICH engagement they describe, so the angle is the lever: frame
  it around a different part of the work.
- Do not close a section the way the other candidates closed theirs, and do not open the summary
  with their wording.

Never invent a product, employer or business domain in order to look different. Vary the telling, not
the facts.
